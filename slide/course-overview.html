Company Setting & Team Rules

Each team runs as a software company

5per company
~20companies
1product / semester

Your team is a company

  • Teams of 5 = a software company (startup), formed in Week 1 with a name and a Company Charter.
  • Assign roles — e.g. Product Owner, Tech Lead, AI Lead, Designer, QA/Test — each person owns a slice (P5). The AI Lead owns AI tooling, disclosure & the AI-ROI metric.
  • Pick your own product/topic; you build it with a standards-based process for real users.

Fulfils the official course description: "simulation of software development company; project proposal and project presentation; standards-based process."

Guardrails — every project must pass all 5

  • Real users ≥ 15 (≈ 3 per member) — no classmates, no AI personas.
  • Buildable in 1 month — exactly 1 core workflow, no more.
  • A measurable metric — you can show a before / after number.
  • PDPA-safe — consent for any real data; no sensitive data without a plan.
  • Not sold / shipped by anyone in the team already — it must be new work.

AI Policy (2569)

AI across the full SDLC — disclosed & governed

✓ Allowed / expected

  • Use AI in every phase — discovery synthesis, coding agents & MCP, test generation, docs
  • AI-generated test cases and mutation testing
  • AI help writing the case study & portfolio
  • Measure AI's ROI on your own SDLC (report it in P4)
  • Must disclose & govern AI usage in P5

✕ Not allowed

  • Fabricating user interviews that never happened
  • Submitting a prototype no real user tested
  • Shipping unreviewed AI output (hallucinations, tech debt)
  • Using AI during the exam

Why start with AI in discovery, not just coding: AI's biggest wins are upstream — requirements, user stories, ideation (problem space), not only coding efficiency. Sources: Gartner, Use AI to Accelerate Productivity Throughout the SDLC (2026); Gartner, P. Walsh, Don't Limit AI in Software Engineering to Coding (2025).
