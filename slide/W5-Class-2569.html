Why this exists

Building the wrong software costs the most

Without a gate
  • Team commits to an idea, not to a user need
  • Builds for a month on a guess
  • Ships something nobody asked for
  • Discovers the real problem in November — too late
With a gate
  • You prove problem–solution fit first
  • Cost of being wrong = a few interviews, not a month
  • BUILD starts with evidence, not opinion
  • This is exactly how industry stage-gates work
💡 In plain terms Failing here, on paper, costs far less than failing in December with a finished product no one wants.

Pillar 1 · Validated user need

Prove the need is real — three paths count

Path A · Interviews

Talk to real users

≥ 5 (target ≥ 15). Dated log + a direct quote of the pain; past behaviour, not "would you use…?".

Path B · User research

Competitors + community

Competitor analysis, app-store reviews, forum / Pantip / social threads — screenshot with source + date.

Path C · Advisor brief

Requirements from อาจารย์

Reqs collected from a faculty advisor / sponsor acting as the client → exempt from the interview count.

⚠ Non-negotiable · every path You still commit to usability testing with real users on your prototype / MVP (run in the TEST phase). No path skips real-user validation — it's just deferred to the usability test. Show a plan: who, what tasks, when.
Still doesn't count: a claim with no evidence behind it, or "users" who are your own teammates. Whichever path — bring the proof.

Pillar 2 · The 4 required diagrams

Present — and consistent

D1

System Context

The system as one box + the actors and systems around it.

D2

Use Case / Story Map

What each actor does — traces to backlog items.

D3

High-level Architecture

The big components + how they connect.

D4

Activity / Sequence

One core workflow, step by step.

Consistent means
  • An actor in D1 appears in D2
  • A use case in D2 has a backlog item
  • A component in D3 does the work in D4
Inconsistent = FAIL
  • D2 shows a feature that's not in the backlog
  • D4 calls a component not drawn in D3
  • Four pretty pictures that don't agree

Pillar 4 · Compliance review

Your W2 law homework becomes a gate check

At the gate, we check your rule.md for
  • PDPA — if you store personal data, is consent + purpose declared?
  • Computer Crime Act §26 — are logs kept ≥ 90 days?
  • Electronic Transactions Act — any e-signature / e-record handled right?
  • Are these written as real backlog requirements, not just prose?
Why now, not later

Compliance is a design input, not something you add later.

"We'll add PDPA later" means rebuilding your data model in October. We catch it here while it's still one line in rule.md.

Link to W2 The rule.md your company wrote in the Week 2 workshop is the file reviewed here — it should sit at repo root next to CLAUDE.md.

How you're judged

Three outcomes — PASS, CONDITIONAL, FAIL

✓ PASS

All four pillars stand. Diagrams present and consistent, need validated (any path), traceability holds, compliance in. BUILD unlocked.

◐ CONDITIONAL

One pillar has a fixable gap (e.g. evidence thin, no usability-test plan, one diagram thin). Fix it this week, re-submit. BUILD starts once cleared.

✕ FAIL

Diagrams inconsistent, no real user evidence, or no traceability. Rework DISCOVER — no BUILD yet.

💡 In plain terms PASS = you can start BUILD. CONDITIONAL = one small fix needed. FAIL = the design is not ready yet. Most gaps are CONDITIONAL, and fixable in days.

Your 5-minute gate pitch

Say these six things — in this order

1 · 30s

The problem

One sentence. Whose pain, and how you know it's real.

2 · 60s

The evidence

Your path — interviews / research / advisor — with one concrete proof + your usability-test plan.

3 · 60s

The trace

Show one pain → requirement → backlog → diagram.

4 · 90s

The 4 diagrams

Show them; say one consistency link between two of them.

5 · 30s

Compliance

One line from rule.md that's now a backlog requirement.

6 · 30s

The ask

"We believe we PASS because…" — name your weakest pillar too.

Tone Not a sales pitch. You are an engineer showing evidence to a reviewer. Admitting your weakest pillar earns more trust than hiding it.

Peer mini-review · how to review another team

Give tough, fair feedback

Good reviewing
  • Score against the scorecard, pillar by pillar
  • Ask "which user asked for this?" on a random feature
  • Name exactly one PASS-blocker — the thing to fix first
  • Point at a diagram inconsistency if you see one
Weak reviewing
  • "Looks good 👍" — helps no one
  • Redesigning their product for them
  • Nit-picking colours instead of checking evidence
  • Criticising the people instead of the work
Hand back A filled scorecard + one sentence: "Your PASS-blocker is ___; fix that first." Score it on the live scorecard → prascymj.github.io/w5-gate-scorecard-2569

The five ways teams miss the gate

Know them, avoid them

The mistakeWhat we checkThe fix
No real evidenceA claim with no log, screenshots, or advisor brief — or no usability-test planShow one path: interviews, captured research, or advisor reqs — + plan the usability test
Solution-firstA cool feature no user ever asked forCut it, or find the pain it answers
Diagrams disagreeD4 uses a component not in D3; D2 ≠ backlogMake one edit ripple to all four + backlog
Compliance as prose"We care about privacy" but no requirementTurn each rule.md rule into a backlog item
Building alreadyProduction code before the gateStop. DISCOVER has no code. That's the point.

If you get CONDITIONAL

It's a to-do list, not a verdict

Your fix-it plan (write it today)
  • The one gap — the pillar that came up amber
  • The concrete fix — e.g. "interview 2 more users", "redraw D3 to match D4"
  • Who + by when — an owner and a re-submit date
Then

Re-submit → cleared → BUILD.

Most teams are CONDITIONAL, not FAIL. A gap found here costs you a couple of days — the same gap found in October costs you the project.
