# Role Statement — Phonepadith Kongsengchanh

**Student ID:** 6631503083  
**Role:** UX/UI Designer

I owned the user experience direction for AdvisingLog. My responsibility was to help turn the advising requirements into screens and flows that are understandable for four different roles: Student, Advisor, Program Chair/QA, and Admin. I focused on keeping the core journey visible: submit a request, schedule the meeting, record the session, assign follow-up work, and review the result. I also considered bilingual Thai/English use, light/dark presentation, readable status labels, and the need to keep sensitive student information within the correct role boundary.

My evidence is the [user journey documentation](../../02-design/user-journey.md), [feature list](../../02-design/feature-list.md), [design system](../../../DESIGN.md), and the role-based pages in the [frontend source](../../../frontend/src/pages/). These artifacts show how the product was organized around real advising tasks rather than only around database entities. I worked with the team to refine the interface after testing feedback identified navigation, text size, notification, and status-feedback issues. The final comparison records both the measured task-time changes and the remaining usability limitations in the [comparison table](../comparison-table.md).

AI was used as a design and documentation assistant for organizing requirements, suggesting alternatives, and explaining implementation options. I treated those suggestions as drafts, checked them against the requirements and implemented screens, and did not use AI to invent user-test results or participant feedback.

### Reflection
## 1. Lessons learned: how did building the app with AI affect software engineering? One thing that got better and one that got worse, from your project.

The Ai has made production speed significantly faster than ever, and with each use we became ever so reliant on it that made manual development a thing of the past. AI has helped us bring our ideas into realityas AGENTS in IDE with a carefully engineered prompting. However, the agent can make mistakes and still needs our commands to direct it.
The Mistakses that AI can make mock data that wasn't insterted manually while testing, which could lead to inconsistency, and misunderstandings during testing phase. Another mistake is Hallucinations which can be caused by lack of context and background to generate the right answers.
What got better is the speed of production in building the project which could taken a year, but instead is reduced to 4 months. The cost is software engineers became more realiant on AI, and lost the ability to confidentally code manually and became less active. When AI generated a UI we asked for, we immediately test out the product to see if its correct, but we did not read the code which can affect maintenance and project expertise when issue arise, and AI is not available to help.
